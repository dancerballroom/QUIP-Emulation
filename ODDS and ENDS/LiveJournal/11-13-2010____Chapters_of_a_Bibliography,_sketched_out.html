I've been told I have a story to tell.
When I look at lessons I've learned.
and the priceless experiences.
It would be a shame to let it all disappear.
I sketch a little some of my stories here.
One day to fully flesh out.

1. My visit to the Reichstag (German Congress, Hitler Museum) in West Germany in 1986

As I closed my eyes and I looked with my minds eye listening to the tone
and the message being spoken, pretending that I didn't know that it was
in fact, Hitler who was delivering a speech, ignoring the knowledge of
atrocities in order to understand what motivated people of an earlier era
to choose war over peace, but rather listening to the message and the tone,
I could hear, "Ask not what your country could do for you, ask what you
can do for your country", and did I shudder as I realized that any proud nation
full of prejudice could also be lead down a similar path with smooth words
behind a hidden agenda.

2. The fall of the Berlin Wall, Before and After

I crossed into East Germany as an American kid in 1986 knowing that East German
people died daily crossing, barbed wire where some might choose to swim,
automatic machine guns where some might choose to run, double walls separated
by a football field of mines, traps, watchtowers with the East German police
called the Stazi (the State's Polizei.) ready to gun down any man, woman, or child
forbidden to cross that border that I as an American could cross for 25 Deutschmark.

The first week of the fall of the wall West Germans were afraid
that their country was going to turn into a 3rd world nation. Lines of
Fluchtlinge(Politically Fleeing Refugees) from East Germany were collecting
their 100 Deutsch Mark (about $60 in 1989). The number one items to be missing
in every super market after the fall of the wall in Berlin were Bananas.
There simply were no countries in the Soviet block that produced bananas and
the first thing they did was go bananas.

3. Hobbies become pursuits in Education: National Choir Award

The best counselor I ever had in high school was my Sophomore counselor. She
was the only one who kept really good records of who I was, what I was doing,
and where I was heading academically. My family had just moved to Gridley, a
small farming town in Butte County, and I had a choice. There were two classes
to choose from. Choir or Agriculture. This was a major turning point in my
choice to pursue music. (Always it seemed to be a choice of Math or Music.)

4. Trombone, Most Improved Section Award up to Music Major

Oh, how I loved to sing. I so believed that this passion would carry over to
an ease of learning an instrument, an ease that never came, but the passion
was so pure. My best friends in high school were all a part of the band,
and the band was like a family. I returned to each day in anticipation.
I think that I was in the band for the sense of family as much as for the
thrill of the music.

5. Latin, a resolution of Never, and the aftermath reality.

Looking back now, I speak fluently German, Spanish, some French. I've Aced
University classes in Japanese, French, German, Spanish, and Latin. I've
personally studied dialects in Swiss German, Platt Deutsch, American Dialects,
and taken bits of Italian, Russian, Dutch, Swedish, and I'm probably not done.

"I will never study a foreign language again for the rest of my life", I said
as I was convinced that I was no good at it, since I had failed my first Latin
class in Freshman High School. The first semester with a "D", and the second
semester with an "F". It didn't help any that I had no idea what studying
was at the time, and that I thought I could simply remember enough without
studying. This method had worked with most everything before, so why not
with Latin? Oh! The things we learn!

6. Walking across campus one day I found a way to visit my Oma in Berlin.

I saw my friend Tom walking across campus with a group of people. I was
in a mood to visit, so I tagged along. Tom was going to a meeting that would
change my life. A visiting professor had started an overseas "International
Education Program". Dr. Seefeld brought students from Europe to California and
from California to Europe. The students would work at department stores, on
farms, in Kiosks, and do reports, earn units, and have an integrated learning
experience.

When I learned of the possibilities I thought of the wonderful door opening
that would allow me to get to know my grandmother. On the few occasions
when she had visited us in the past I had not been able to communicate with
her, as she only spoke German and French.

Dr. Seefeldt said I didn't have enough German to go to Germany, so I decided to
go to the Canary Islands the first year with Germany as my second target, and
oh my goodness, I did it, but boy, I learned a lot in the process.

7. Youth Conservation Corp, A Pick-Axe and an electrical mood adjustment

As I excitedly swung my pick axe with enthusiasm, having built a cabin, a
fire-pit, an out-house, a trail, and now digging the horse-shoe troth, I heard
the now familiar, "clink, pzzt, tchhhhhhhhhhhhhh". I had once again, now for
the third time, broken another water line with my trusty pick-axe. Fortunately
for me, no one thought that I did it on purpose, but there was a lot of
ribbing that evening by the crew and I think they remembered me long
afterwords as well for my uncanny ability to find the water lines under the
earth.

Another evening as I was checking on the state of the electric generator
after having been extremely agitated and angry about something ( I have no
memory of the reason now ), the light from the sun was disappearing, and the
trail to the distant generator was practically dark from the towering trees of
the forest. I reached down to the generator to adjust the knobs and
ZZZAAAAPPPP! I found myself sitting a bit away from the generator with a totally
mellow sense of centered-ness and happiness of being alive. I just gave myself
an electric shock treatment, and I wasn't dead. Cool!

8. Porter in a Hotel. Merry Christmas.

Meredith Daub was from New York. Merry was studying German with a group
of Americans when I was teaching the TOEFL course for students wanting to
take the Test Of English as a Foreign Language in order to study at English
speaking universities. I told Merry that I wasn't sure what I'd do for a job
once I got back to Chico, CA. She suggested that I turn in an application for
a position of Porter at a Holiday Inn. This was about 1 1/2 years before I
got that position. The hotel management were surprised that I had applied,
since they never advertised for this position. I eventually did every job
there was to be done in a Hotel over a period of four years.

9. Advent in the Canary Islands

"What have I done?" I said, as I looked into the mirror. Knowing well that
I could not understand a word being said this entire first day having arrived
in the Canary Islands on a Sunday, no one knowing of my arrival, borrowing
money from a Hare Krishna in order to phone my coordinator.

This turned out to be one of the most wonderful experiences I've had in my
whole life. It was in the canary islands I learned what it is really like
to fall in love with learning languages. Priceless experience.

10. 12 Units of "A" or else. Trip fantastique du Master's Degree

Have you ever felt like you were constantly fighting an uphill battle where
every obstacle that appeared seemed to have multiple parts? This was my
experience in obtaining a master's degree in computer science.

I had a plethora of sordid grades, both excelling in classes, and occasionally being
mediocre if I didn't like the instructor or if I was forced to take a subject
that was exceedingly difficult. Several times I took classes over again in
order to succeed. Calculus and Advanced Computer Math Theory kicked my ass.

In any event, every door has it's watcher. My door guard was in the Graduate
Office. She encouraged me to excel by letting me know that I needed at least
12 Units of "A" in order to graduate with the necessary GPA for the masters.

Being scared can be an incredible motivator if you have enough time to prepare.
I aced 14 units. I graduated with my Master's degree. I told my friends I
would graduate after I was 40. Well, it only took 11 years after all.

11. A scary trip to Mexico

As I looked down the car husk ridden mountain side of a one lane blind
road without guard rails as we climbed and climbed, no turn-outs and no
safety curbs in sight, with fast moving trucks flying up and down the road,
I remembered having been very lucky this trip to Mexico. My friend Todd
and I chose to cross from Ensenada to San Felipe across a desert road not
maintained like regular toll highway. On top of only entering Mexico with
a meager $200.00 each, we both decided on this adventure to practice our
Spanish. The policeman who pulled us over for speeding let us go because
he felt so bad for us.

Then there was the Fireworks Fiasco. Our big purile plan was to buy and
blow up illegal fireworks in Mexico. We waited til dark on a wide coastline
with no one close by and lots and lots of sand. After blowing up our fireworks
it was really dark. "I can't find the key's'" said Todd, as we were just about
to leave. It was still only about 9pm, and we had dropped the keys somewhere
along a strip of 1/4 mile of sand. The only other key was in Los Angeles with
Todd's mom. We searched and searched and searched. About 2 hours later we saw
a glimmer in the sand. The bare end of a key stuck out of the sand where we had
walked to the beach.
