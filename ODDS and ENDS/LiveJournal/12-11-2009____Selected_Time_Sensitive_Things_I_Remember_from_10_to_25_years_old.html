12-11-2009    Selected Time Sensitive Things I Remember from 10 to 25 years old

  • Dec. 11th, 2009 at 1:38 AM
This is a continuation of my last post. As I look back on my past knowing that each experience is common to my contemporaries and yet singular to my own experience I wonder what my footprint on humanity will be. To know thine own past is to know thine self and others.

From 1974-1989. I remember:

1. the first TV console our family purchased based on integrated circuit technology. The old TVs had vacuum tubes and a couple of those were in black and white.

2. taking a typing class as a sophomore in high school in Gridley, CA. (My father, who had worked for years in a state job said he didn't learn to type until after he retired at 55 (playing with the computer), because when he was going to school the philosophy was that typists would be the only ones required to type)

3. taking driver's education as a sophomore in high school (the other half a year in the same classroom was a health and sex ed. class.

4. "Most Hard Working" award in the Youth Conservation Corps (1980)

5. "Most Improved Section" award in Marching Band (1981)

6. "National Choir" award in High School Chorus (1982)

7. thinking that the United States was the best nation on earth because it was guided by great thinking leaders that would put the betterment of the people above self serving interest groups. (I keep hope up.)

8. believing that all things were possible to those who work hard enough. In other words, I believed that good will, faith, and good works would be rewarded either by man or by God. (My grandfather retired from the New York Times after over 40 years of dedicated service. (His first job after college) My father retired from Social Services after 25 years of dedicated services. (His first major job after college). My two uncles retired in a similar fashion from their professions that they started shortly after their educations: National Forestry Service, and the Oregonian Newspaper. (I am 45, have worked for at least 10 minimum wage establishments, and at one career job at Pacific Bell (that I left for furthering my education))

9. traveling on work programs through the Foothill College International Education Program (IEP) run by Guenther Seefeldt from 1985-1990. Kiosk in Canary Islands(Spanish, 1985), Farm in West Germany(Germany, 1986), MIGROS Supermarket in Switzerland(Swiss German, 1988). (God I love Switzerland so much I wish I could have lived there longer), Del Haize Supermarche in Bruxelles(French, 1988)

10. visiting my grandmother 1986-1987 Frau Johanna Rollke who lived in Berlin (Wedding Bezirk) taking classes learning German at the Goethe Institute. I lived there for 9 months.

11. (1989) working in an exchange program at Eberhardt-Karls University of Tuebingen (Prepping students for the TOEFL, a test of English as a Foreign Language exam for entering English speaking universities).

12. Visiting west Berlin 3 days after the fall of the Berlin Wall. (1989) (The West Germans I talked to said that they were worried about a general decay of their standard of living with the opening of East Germany).

13. my roommate (1983) at CSUC, Chico CA, Todd Hoover, taking "Assembly Language" classes requiring programming in assembly language on punch cards.

14. the explosion of the Challenger shuttle in 1986 (sitting next to my roommate's 100 gallon aquarium in a lazy boy recliner after returning home from classes.)
