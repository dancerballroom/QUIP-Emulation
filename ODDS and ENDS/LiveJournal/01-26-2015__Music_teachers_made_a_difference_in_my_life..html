01-26-2015  Music teachers made a difference in my life.

  • Jan. 26th, 2015 at 9:53 AM
My Fantastic Music Teacher who changed my life.

Constantly moving from place to place from the ages of 5 and 16, I arrived in Gridley, CA with my mom for my Sophomore year in High School. It was 1978-1980. Gridley was a very small community, mostly migrant workers and farmers. I had arrived a week before school started due to an opening in Butte County Housing and the fact our landlord was selling the house we were renting in Oroville.

The only two elective classes Gridley High School offered that weren't full were 1. an Ag class, and 2. Choir. I took a chance and signed up for Choir. The Choir was small: 3 boys and 8 girls. We had a limited number of songs, and the music wasn't particularly complicated or professional. I remember the boys singing: "Good night Ladies", while the girls sang: "O, when the saints go marching in." and the like.

This year was so good for me because I found out I loved music. Some people said I was too old to start a music profession. It still doesn't matter that I don't have a music profession today. I know from my friend's experience that It's a very tough field even if you are a fantastic musician. I love to play, compose, remaster Karaoke, and record Audio Books. I have no regrets learning music.

"Dan Soares" was my teacher's name. I don't think he ever knew how much I loved him. He had no accompanyist, so he plunked out the accompanyment himself. He smiled a lot and he had an infectious ability to make you smile and laugh. Every day I would look forward to his class and I always thought the hour went too fast. I would forget my worries, be happy and fascinated by the music, and found a profound connection to happiness.

Since then I have been exposed to many teachers, a few excellent teachers, and a very few (but priceless) fantastic teachers. "Dan Soares" was one of my first fantastic teachers. He wasn't exceptionally musically skilled, but he changed my life.
