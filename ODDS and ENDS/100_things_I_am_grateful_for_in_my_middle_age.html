  • In the maelstrom of a life full of moving hither and thither a certain amount of permanence and reliability of always having a place I could call home.  There was one time in Germany I was homeless and it is something that I am grateful I haven’t had to experience too many times.  During an flood warning evacuation of the Oroville Dam a few years back my nephew Tim helped us out with a place to stay.  Thank God for the wonderful people in our lives.
  • My wife helping me with so many things.  She is my co-pilot, my friend, my love, and my support.   
  • Concerts and get-together’s.  Games and projects.  Walks in the park.  And the occasional voyage to other cities and places.
  • A spiritual life.  Although I don’t have the traditional church going history, my family didn’t visit church at all, except my mom in her later years.  I always felt a close connection to God, although I did not profess to be a Christian until the 1990’s at the Neighborhood Church many years later when I was in their Choir.  I was interested in the Bible because people would challenge me and tell me what I believed, so I joined the Crusade for Christ at Butte College in the 1980’s to study it more thoroughly.  This led to an interest of eastern and western religion, visiting various types of non protestant churches (Catholic, Mormon, Eckinkar, Scientology, etc.).  Church for me is a private thing between me and my creator and Church is a way to nourish that connection, much like experiencing the power of the ocean, the beauty of the sky and the invigorating trip on a bicycle through a beautiful park.  I am awestruck.
  • Reaching out and making friends.  It’s not easy to make friends.  I cherish those who make the permanent friends list and appreciate my great acquaintances.  I have my groups:  Karaoke, Lion’s, University, High School, both students and professors, Overseas acquaintances, Church groups from various churches, Neighbors, both previous and current, my Work groups, from all the businesses I’ve worked for, and my extended family group, my wife’s friends and family.  And the list goes on and on.  Some fade away and many remember my name before I remember theirs but I love them all.
  • Learning success through constant repetitive work.  Many failures that I may have had in the past were more situational than a reflection of my abilities.  I also accept that I am slower than most at taking notes and sometimes even though I know my subject I do not finish in the time allotted.  I do however have a B.A. in Music, a M.S. in Computer Science, and I have a fluency in German and Spanish, and a smattering of other languages.  I failed Latin in my Freshman year in HS, but aced it several years later at University.  Ear Training and Calculus kicked my butt.  However I did pass the final Ear Training exam and second semester of Calculus.  It just took years instead of semesters.  “Fake it till you make it.”  I totally believe in this saying.
  • The YCC “Youth Conservation Corps” was a subset of the CCC “California Conservation Corps”.  My first time away from home at 16 years old and lessons which I take with me for a lifetime about hard work, self reliance, independence, working together, and watching where the water main is.  I managed to pick-axe the water main 3 times while building cabins and trails.  I was a regular dowsing rod.  Convincing my son William to try it out made me proud.
  • Helping my mom pay off her student loans.  Sometimes we all need help and it’s a wonderful feeling when you can help your parents out.  She also paid me back over time.
  • Knowing that my parents loved me.  That made all the difficulties survivable no matter what happened.
